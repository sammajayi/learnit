# LearnIt curriculum: A0 to A2

Last updated: 26 November 2026. Applies to French and Spanish. Both courses follow the same unit map, so a unit number means the same thing in either language.

## Status

**Rule for every change to this course:** units 1 to 8 are live. Their lesson, word and checkpoint ids never change, because learners' progress is stored under them, and new work only adds units after them. Regenerating content must leave units 1 to 8 byte-for-byte identical to main.

## What each level means

We use the CEFR scale. The goals below are "can do" statements. A learner who finishes a level should be able to do them, and each unit's checkpoint tests the language that supports them.

### A0: first contact

The learner can recognise and use a few dozen everyday words and fixed phrases.

- Greet people, be polite and say goodbye.
- Count to 100 and name days, colours and times of day.
- Say their name, where they are from and which languages they speak.
- Name close family and common people words.
- Order a drink or a simple meal and say what they like.

### A1: getting by

The learner can understand and use familiar everyday phrases and very simple sentences.

- Ask for and give directions to common places.
- Say what they do each day and what they need.
- Describe people, things, feelings and the weather in one short sentence.
- Buy things, ask the price and pay.
- Ask for help and ask someone to speak more slowly.

### A2: simple, connected conversations

The learner can handle routine situations and talk about the past, the near future and their daily life in short, connected sentences.

- Say what they did yesterday or last weekend.
- Make, accept and decline plans.
- Describe a daily routine and how often they do things.
- Explain a health problem at a pharmacy or doctor's.
- Talk about work and study, and make a phone call or appointment.
- Handle a travel problem such as a delay, a lost item or a change of booking.
- Compare things and give a simple opinion.
- Tell a short story with first, then, because and but.

## How a unit works

Every regular unit has 5 themes. Each theme is split into two short lessons (part 1 and part 2) of 4 new words each. The parts below are in the order the learner meets them.

| Step | What it is | New words? |
|---|---|---|
| Word card | "Meet the words first": the 4 new words, tap to hear each one, plus a grammar tip on part 1 where the theme has one | Yes, always |
| Part 1 lesson | 6 questions on the 4 new words | Yes |
| Part 2 lesson | Word card for the next 4 words, then an essay question, then 6 questions | Yes |
| Story time | One short story lesson at the end of the unit, before the checkpoint | No |
| Checkpoint | 20 questions drawn from a pool of 60 | No |

### The word card rule

A lesson opens with the word card only when it teaches new words. Story lessons, revision lessons and checkpoints never open with one. So the learner is only asked to stop and study words when there are words to study, and review lessons start straight on the questions.

### Essays

One essay per theme, so 5 per unit. It is the first question of part 2, after that lesson's word card.

- **Language:** the learner's target language only. No English anywhere in the essay or its options.
- **Length:** 2 or 3 short sentences, about 12 to 30 words.
- **How it works:** the text is read aloud when the question opens, with a button to hear it again. The learner then picks what it is about from 4 options, also in the target language (1 to 4 words each). The prompt around it ("What is this text about?") is interface text in English.
- **Vocabulary:** only words the learner has met so far, including the 8 words of the current theme.
- **Why it sits in part 2:** essays are comprehension practice, not a way to earn. Putting one inside part 2 keeps them out of the lesson, reward and streak counts, so a one-question essay can never be farmed.
- **Volume:** 5 per unit, so 50 per language for units 1 to 10 and 80 per language at 16 units.

Example (Spanish, unit 4, theme "Drinks"): "Me gusta el café. Por la mañana bebo café con leche. Por la noche bebo agua." Options: "bebidas", "la casa", "el tiempo", "mi familia". The topic words must be words the learner knows; if they are not, use a shorter phrase that they do.

### Story time

One short story per unit, as a normal lesson. It is lesson 11 of the unit and is required before the checkpoint.

- **Text:** 4 to 6 short sentences in the target language, read aloud. A character the learner can follow (for example, a person going to the market, or a day in the life of a student), using the unit's words and earlier ones.
- **English:** hidden by default. A "Show English" control reveals the translation after the learner has tried reading it. This is a support for A0 and A1 learners and can be removed if you want stories fully immersive too.
- **Questions:** 5 multiple-choice comprehension questions, each with 4 options. The prompts and options are in English so the test is understanding, not reading speed. Pass at 80%, like every lesson.
- **No word card:** a story teaches no new words.
- **Counts as a lesson:** it earns a reward, league points and a streak tick like any other lesson, and the 3 lessons a day limit still applies.

### Revision units

The last section of each level is a revision unit. It teaches nothing new.

| Revision unit | Comes after | Revises | Words covered (at 16 units) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A0 revision | Unit 3 | Units 1 to 3 | 120 |
| A1 revision | Unit 8 | Units 4 to 8 | 200 |
| A2 revision | Unit 16 | Units 9 to 16 | 320 |

- **Built from the level's own content.** Questions are drawn from that level's words and sentences, so there is nothing new to write or review.
- **Shape:** 10 lessons of 6 questions (mixed multiple choice, matching and sentence building) and a checkpoint. The checkpoint pool covers every word in the level, so the learner is tested on all of it even though each lesson samples a part.
- **No word cards.** Lessons start straight on the questions.
- **Missed words:** words a learner gets wrong are fed back into later revision lessons so the practice is aimed at what they find hard.
- **Label:** shown on the path as "Revision", not as a numbered unit, so unit numbers 1 to 16 stay the same.
- **A2 revision waits for unit 16.** Until the A2 units are all written it would move every time one is added, so it is built when the A2 level is complete.

## Grammar progression

Grammar is taught through short tips on the word card of part 1. A tip is a title and at most 3 short sentences with one or two examples. The tip never asks the learner to memorise a table.

| Level | Introduced | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | Fixed phrases, I am and I have, I like, yes and no | Units 1 to 3 as chunks, no tips yet |
| A1 | Present tense in common verbs, question words, negation, adjectives | Units 4 to 8 as vocabulary and sentences, no tips yet |
| A2 | Past tense of common verbs, going to, can and want to plus a verb | Units 9 and 10, with tips |
| A2 | Frequency words, reflexive verbs, have to and should, comparatives, connectors | Units 11 to 16, with tips |

French and Spanish differ here. French uses the passé composé for the past (with avoir or être), Spanish the pretérito indefinido. Imperfect forms appear only as fixed chunks in A2 (for example the weather or "I was tired") and are not taught as a system. Stories at A2 may use the past tense the learner has been taught.

## Vocabulary targets

Every unit teaches 40 new words or chunks (5 themes of 8) and 15 sentences (3 per theme). Two rules keep multiple-choice questions fair:

- No two items in a unit share the same target-language text or the same English meaning.
- Each theme is split into two lessons of 4 words. A tip, if any, shows on part 1.

| | Regular units | Words per language | Sentences per language | Essays per language | Stories per language |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today (built) | 10 | 400 | 150 | 0 | 0 |
| With essays and stories for units 1 to 10 | 10 | 400 | 150 | 50 | 10 |
| At 16 units | 16 | 640 | 240 | 80 | 16 |

Essays and stories reuse words already taught, so they add reading practice without raising the word count.

## Known gaps

1. **Vocabulary size.** The CEFR A2 level is usually associated with around 1,000 words. At 640 curated words and chunks, the course covers the A2 situations above but not the full vocabulary. Extending past unit 16 (for example, a unit each on food in depth, housing, free time and culture) would close this. Not committed.
2. **No grammar tips in A0 and A1.** Units 1 to 8 teach through words and sentences only. Adding tips to units 4 to 8 is a small backfill.
3. **Reading and listening only.** Lessons test reading, listening and building sentences. They do not test speaking or free writing. The AI chat and voice practice on the roadmap are the planned answer.
4. **Level exams.** Each unit has a checkpoint and each level ends with a revision unit, but there is no separate exam. Worth deciding before anyone is told they have "reached A2".
5. **No native-speaker review.** Decided 26 Nov 2026: we do not need a native speaker to review the A2 units, essays or stories. The list of uncertain points at the top of each source file is for our own spot checks.

## Decisions to confirm

These are choices I made while specifying the changes. Each is easy to reverse.

1. **Essay inside part 2, graded.** Because the essay is the first question of part 2, it counts toward that lesson's 80% pass mark. The alternative is an ungraded reading break between the two parts, which needs its own screen and is not saved.
2. **Story counts as a normal lesson.** It earns rewards and league points. If you would rather stories gave no reward, they need a flag so they are skipped when rewards and points are calculated.
3. **Learners who finished a unit before stories exist.** Their path will show the new story lesson as outstanding. The unit stays passed, but the story shows as next. There are only a few users today, so the effect is small.
4. **English reveal on stories.** Essays have no English at all. Stories hide English behind "Show English". Remove it if stories should be target language only.
5. **What "mandated to revise words" means.** I read it as: the word card shows only when a lesson has new words, so nothing forces a word review on lessons that have none. If you meant something else, for example making learners repeat words they got wrong, tell me and I will change the rule.

## How the content is built

- Source: `backend/scripts/a1/{fr,es}.ts` (units 1 to 8) and `backend/scripts/a2/{fr,es}.ts` (units 9 onwards). The A2 files use the same helpers.
- Helpers: `U` unit, `T` theme, `S` sentence, `G` attaches a grammar tip to a theme.
- Check: `npx tsx scripts/check-units.ts a1|a2 fr|es` (from `backend/`) validates the shape and catches duplicates and dash characters.
- Generate: `npm run content` writes `backend/src/content/{fr,es}/unit{N}.json` and `index.ts`. Do not edit the JSON.
- Level is stored per unit (`A0`, `A1` or `A2`) and shown on the learning path.
- **Ids never change once published.** Learner progress is stored under lesson and checkpoint ids. Units 1 to 8 keep their original `a1` ids even though units 1 to 3 now show as A0. New A2 units use `a2` ids. Revision units and stories will use their own id patterns so they never collide.

## Copy standard

All lesson text, tips and in-app copy follow the LearnIt voice in the design system (Voice page) and the copy skill:

- British English (colour, practise as the verb).
- No em dashes or en dashes.
- Short sentences, one idea each, numerals for numbers.
- Warm, calm and honest. No hype, no money-win language.
- Glosses are the natural English meaning, not a word-for-word translation, and mark polite or informal forms where it matters.
- Essays and stories are in natural target-language prose. Use proper French and Spanish punctuation in them (« » with narrow no-break spaces, ¿ and ¡), as the voice guide allows for lesson content.

## Adding a unit

1. Write the unit in the language's source file (5 themes, 8 words and 3 sentences each).
2. Add a tip with `G(...)` whenever a theme introduces a grammar pattern.
3. Write one essay per theme and one story for the unit, using only words taught so far.
4. Run the check script until it prints `ok`.
5. Run `npm run content`, then `npm test` and `npm run check`.
6. Spot-check the target-language text ourselves with the unit checker (`npx tsx scripts/check-units.ts a2 fr|es`) before release.
7. Update the status table in this file.
